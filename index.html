Diploma thesis

Puzzle Assembly via a Robotic Manipulator

In progressPythonOpenCVDeep learningRobotics

A system that photographs the pieces of a jigsaw puzzle, works out the solution from the shape of the pieces alone, and will have a robotic arm assemble it. The vision and solving pipeline now works end to end. Below, one piece of a real 9-piece puzzle goes through every step.

images/thesis-1-photo.jpgphoto of a piece
01

Photograph the pieces

Each piece is photographed from above, from the same height and under the same lighting, so that every side length is measured on the same scale.

images/thesis-2-segmented.webppiece without background
02

Remove the background

IS-Net locates the piece in a downscaled copy of the photo, then segments it again on a full-resolution crop. Each contour point is then snapped to the strongest image edge with sub-pixel accuracy. Of the three models I compared (U²-Net, IS-Net, BiRefNet), IS-Net gave the most accurate contours, at under half a second per piece.

images/thesis-3-isthmus.jpgisthmuses, corners & sides
03

Isthmuses, corners & sides

Following Webster et al. (1991), the skeleton of the piece gives the necks of its tabs (green) and the skeleton of the background around it gives the mouths of its blanks (red). With the tabs cut off at their necks, the convex hull of the remaining shape gives the four corners. Each side is then labelled tab, blank or flat, and sanity checks flag any piece that looks wrong.

images/thesis-4-solved.jpgsolved puzzle
04

Solve the puzzle

The solver places the border first, then the interior, and backtracks when it gets stuck. Two sides are joined only if they pass two independent checks: the isthmus check from the paper (neck vs. mouth width, isthmus distance functions) and a side check (equal length, curves that coincide after rigid ICP alignment). Real 9-piece and 20-piece puzzles are solved with every join passing both checks.

Next: better image capture (a backlight panel and camera calibration), then converting the solution to robot coordinates and assembling the puzzle with a suction gripper.

Projects

Optimal Football Lineup Selection

2024 · Python · Gurobi · Binary Integer Programming

Course project for Linear & Combinatorial Optimization. Choosing a starting XI is an assignment problem, so I modeled it as one for Olympiacos FC's 2023–24 squad.

9 / 11starters matched the lineup actually fielded in the 2024 UEFA Conference League final
  • 42 binary player–position variables from a 27-player squad, with formation, positional and single-selection constraints
  • Player ratings built from position-weighted Football Manager 2024 attributes combined with real SofaScore match ratings
  • Solved and compared 5 formations (4-2-3-1 scored best)
  • Integer programs have no shadow prices, so I wrote my own sensitivity analysis: rating tolerance intervals for each starter and the best replacement for each absent player
